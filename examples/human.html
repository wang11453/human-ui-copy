How the split actually works

  1. Read the page tree first. A PDF is not a flat list of pages; it's a tree with a catalog at the root. Splitting means copying the page objects and rewriting their parents, otherwise the output opens fine in one viewer and is broken in the next.
  2. Copy, don't re-encode. Pages are copied as objects, so text stays selectable and images keep their original quality. A re-encoded split would drop 40% of the file size and all of the bookmarks.
  3. Then keep the outlines. Bookmarks that point into a kept page range are rewritten; the rest are dropped rather than left dangling.

Scanned PDFs are images, so "split" can only cut them at page boundaries; text selection and search won't appear after the split. This is a limit of the source file, not of the tool.
